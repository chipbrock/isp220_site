# The Cosmologies of Galileo and Newton

## Example 5: in a plane

**The Question:** 

The figure below shows my favorite Delta jet flying 5 miles above the surface of the earth, so 3965 miles from the center of the earth. If you drop your apple in the plane (or peanuts…because who can get those little packages open?) what fraction of $g$ (the on-earth value) does it feel that far from the center of the earth?

<img align="center" src="./tree.png">

<BR CLEAR="all">

**The Answer:**

$$
a(\text{plane}) = G\cfrac{M}{(R_E + 5\text{ miles})^2}.
$$

Ask Mr. Google and you’ll find that that $R_E$ is more than 17 million feet and 5 miles is $5\times 5280 = 26,400$ feet. So we have:

$$
\begin{align*}
a(\text{plane}) &= G\cfrac{M}{(17,000,000 + 26,400\text{ feet})^2} \\
a(\text{plane}) &= G\cfrac{M}{(17,026,400\text{ feet})^2}
\end{align*}
$$

$$
g=G\frac{M}{R_E^2} = 32; a(plane)
$$

Let's form the ratio of $a(plane)$ to $g$:

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{a(\text{plane})}{g} &= \frac{ 
G\frac{M}{17,026,400^2}
}{
G\frac{M}{17,000,000^2}
}  \\
&= \frac{17,000,000^2}{17,026,400^2} \\
\frac{a(\text{plane})}{g} &= 0.9984
\end{align*}
$$
