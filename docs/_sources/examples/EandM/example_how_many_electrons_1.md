# Faraday's E&M Fields

## Example 1: How Many Electrons? 

**The Question:** 

For that total charge of a single micro-Coulomb on our pith ball, $10^{-9}$ Coulombs, how many electrons would would it take to make that tiny amount of charge?

**The Answer:**

The charge of a single electron is
$$
e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}
$$
otherwise known as...not very much.

So the number of electrons is the total charge divided by the charge per electron:
$$
\begin{align*}
N_e &= \frac{Q_\text{total}}{e} \\
    &= \frac{1 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} \\
    &= 0.625 \times 10^{-6-(-19)} = 0.625 \times 10^{-6+19)} \\
N_e &=0.625 \times 10^{13} = 6.25 \times 10^{12}
\end{align*}
$$
which is 6.25 trillion electrons.
